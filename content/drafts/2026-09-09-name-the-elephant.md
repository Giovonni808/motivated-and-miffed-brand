# Draft set 2, enriched (2026-09-09)

**Deficit:** click rate 0.35% against 1.5%. 253 opens, one click on the last issue.
**Anchors:** all present-tense, from the 2026-09-09 interview. No résumé material in this set.
**Format constraint applied:** every piece below needs zero new capture. Time is the binding
constraint and the assets are scattered, so a format requiring fresh footage would not get made.

**The find:** the Eat the Frog Reel published to Instagram and TikTok on 2025-09-20 is a documented,
verifiable reversal of a position now held. Reversing your own published advice is the strongest
short-form opening available, and it costs nothing to shoot because the old post is the b-roll.

## 1. Reel and TikTok: the reversal

**Format:** credibility anchor then payload. Screen recording of the old post, text overlay.
**Effort:** under an hour. No new footage.
**Length:** ~40 seconds.

**HOOK (0-3s). On screen: the actual 2025-09-20 post, scrolling.**
> "Last September I posted a video telling you to eat the frog. I was wrong."

**Body (3-14s)**
> Eat the frog says do the hardest thing first. Fine. It assumes you already know what the frog is.
>
> Most of the time I don't. I know I'm avoiding something. I don't know what.

**Turn (14-26s)**
> The problem is almost never time and it's almost never resources. It's that I don't have the
> willpower to do the thing I know needs doing. Eating a frog doesn't fix that. It just picks a task.

**Payload (26-38s)**
> So here's the version I use now. Name the elephant.
>
> The frog is the task. The elephant is the reason you haven't touched it.
>
> Once it has a name you can blame it, shame it, attack it. Or heal from it. You can't do any of that
> to something you haven't named.

**CTA (38-45s)**
> I broke down how I run this in this week's Motivated and Miffed, the issue called "You're Not in
> Flow. You're Just Grinding." Link in bio.

**Production notes:** open on a screen recording of the old frog post so the reversal is visual before
it is spoken. Everything after that can be text on a dark plate. Dynamic captions. Nothing here needs
a camera.

## 3. LinkedIn: the permanence argument

**Format:** contrarian workflow take. Plain text, no hashtag block.

> I paid for Notion's AI add-on back when it cost extra. I do not think I have opened Notion since.
>
> It was not that Notion is bad. It is that I had to learn its language to use it properly, and I kept
> catching myself asking what happens to that investment if the product changes, or the pricing changes,
> or I do.
>
> So I moved to Obsidian, which is a folder of plain text files sitting on my machine. I am still
> learning it. It might not stick either, and I am aware of that.
>
> But the rule I came out with is the part that transfers: before you learn a tool's vocabulary, ask
> whether the vocabulary outlives the tool.
>
> Learning keyboard shortcuts is cheap. Learning a proprietary way of thinking about your own work is
> not, and nobody prices that in when they are comparing software.
>
> Ten years in production taught me the same lesson in a different room. Every editing suite invents its
> own names for the same three operations. The people who switch fastest are the ones who learned the
> operations rather than the menus.
>
> It took a Notion subscription to teach me that this applies to how I think, not just how I cut.
>
> I write about this kind of thing weekly in Motivated and Miffed, for creatives with actual deadlines.

## 4. Static post or text-list Reel: the consistency admission

**Format:** static b-roll with text list. Highest-performing pattern in the pull at 234x its creator's
median, and the cheapest thing here to make.
**Effort:** well under an hour.

**Hook, on screen:**
> "I write a newsletter about building systems for consistency. My own content folder has been empty for
> weeks."

**Body:**
> The system is not the problem. I built it and it is fine. A folder in Drive, color-coded, green means
> ready to work on. Each project is its own folder with the image or the video and the caption already
> written.
>
> It works. I stopped feeding it.
>
> Not because it was complicated. Because work drained me and rest won. That is the real reason, and
> building a better system was never going to fix it.
>
> So what I am changing is not the folder. It is where the folder lives, so I can add to it from my phone
> in the ten minutes I actually have, instead of the two hours I keep pretending I will find.

**CTA:**
> That is the kind of thing I write about every week in Motivated and Miffed. This week: why grinding
> keeps getting mistaken for focus. Link in bio.

**Honest flags.**

The reversal is the strongest asset in this set and it is also spendable once. It works because it is
the first time. Do not build a habit of retracting posts for engagement, because the second one reads
as a bit.

Draft 4 is the most exposed thing here. It admits publicly that the newsletter's author struggles with
the newsletter's subject. That is why it will perform, and it is also a real disclosure. Worth a
deliberate yes rather than a default one.

The carousel caption names the folder problem as "embarrassingly small." That is a judgement about his
own work, not a documented fact. Cut it if it lands wrong.

"Name the elephant" is a strong enough frame to be a recurring M&M format rather than one post. If it
gets traction, it belongs in the brand vocabulary table in `gio-brand-voice` alongside "1% improvements"
and "Table It, Don't Trash It".
