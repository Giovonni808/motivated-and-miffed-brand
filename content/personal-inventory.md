lost: the newsletter is about building systems for consistency, and the consistency is the part
failing.

bottleneck is that when he sits down, **everything he needs is not in one place.**

  Right now it is not.

  at the moment, and he knows it.

**The system he actually built for it:** a "new material" folder inside the Motivated & Miffed Google
Drive. Color-coded, where green means ready to work on now. Each project is a folder holding an image
or a video plus text for context or a social caption.

**Why it stalled:** he has fallen by the wayside on populating it, even though the structure is
straightforward. In his words, he lost track of time, and "work has been making me want to relax and
rest a lot more."

That last part matters and should not be sanded off. The system did not fail because it was badly
designed. It failed because the job drained him and rest won. That is the honest version and it is
more useful than a tooling story.

## Advice I think is wrong

**2026-09-09 : Eat the frog. Replace it with: name the elephant.**

His position: Eat the Frog is useful for figuring out what you actually need to work on. Taken at face
value it is bound to fail, because the reason you have a productivity problem is usually not time and
not resources. It is that you do not have the willpower to do the thing you feel needs doing.

His replacement framing: **name the white elephant in the room.** Name the elephant rather than eat
the frog. Most of the time we do not know the small things stopping us from getting where we want to
go. Once it is named, in his words, you can "name, blame, shame, or attack it. Or heal from it."

The sharp articulation, built from his reasoning: eat the frog assumes you already know what the frog
is. The elephant is the reason you have not touched it.

Note for drafting: he published Eat the Frog advice himself on 2025-09-20, so the reversal is his own
and documented. That makes it usable without punching at anyone else.
